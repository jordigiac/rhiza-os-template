# Structural Tells — the StoryScope pass

The 33 patterns in SKILL.md are prose-level tells. This file covers the layer above them: the *shape* of the piece. The StoryScope study (University of Maryland + Google DeepMind, arXiv:2604.03136, 61,608 stories) found AI writing is detectable from structure alone with 93% accuracy — and when researchers style-edited the text to remove every prose tell, detection barely dropped. You cannot prose-edit your way out of a structural fingerprint. The structure has to be fixed at the structure level.

The study measured fiction, but the fingerprints map directly onto marketing copy, emails, scripts, and posts. Run this pass BEFORE the prose-level pass — structure decisions shape sentences, not the other way around.

## The five structural fingerprints

### 1. Over-explained takeaway
AI states its theme outright 77% of the time; humans, 52%. In copy this is the closing paragraph that spells out the lesson: "The takeaway? Consistency beats motivation." If the piece earned its point, the reader already has it. Cut the moral. End on the concrete thing — the image, the number, the last step — and let the reader do the final inch of work. If the piece *didn't* earn its point without the summary, the body is the problem, not the ending.

### 2. Tidy, single-track arc
AI favors one clean thread where everything introduced gets used and everything raised gets resolved. Human writing wanders a little: an aside that doesn't pay off, a second thread left hanging, a detail included just because it's true. In copy: don't make every paragraph a stepping stone to the CTA. Let one thing exist for texture. A loose end is a human signature.

### 3. Flat, even escalation
AI escalates in uniform steps — each section slightly more intense than the last, like a ramp. Humans spike and idle: long flat stretch, sudden jump, digression, then the point. In copy: vary the emotional altitude between sections. If you plotted intensity per paragraph and got a straight line, redraw it.

### 4. No mixed feelings
AI frames choices as clean — right option, wrong option, clear stakes. Humans hedge, hold two feelings at once, and admit when something mostly-works-but-bothers-them. In copy: "I love this protocol and I still skip it twice a week" is more credible than "this protocol changed everything." Unresolved tension reads as honesty because it is.

### 5. Linear time
AI tells it in order: past → present → future. Humans start in the middle, flash back, jump ahead, circle back. In copy: opening at the moment of failure and backfilling how you got there beats opening with "Six months ago I started..."

## How to run the pass

Before drafting (or when auditing a draft), answer these five questions:

1. Does the ending explain the point the body already made? → cut it.
2. Does every element feed the single thread? → let one thing be texture.
3. Is the intensity a smooth ramp? → flatten a stretch, spike a moment.
4. Is every claim a clean take? → find the one honest hedge and keep it.
5. Is it told strictly in order? → try starting at the most interesting moment instead.

You don't need all five in every piece — a 100-word caption may only have room for one. But a piece that fails all five is structurally AI-shaped no matter how human the sentences sound.
