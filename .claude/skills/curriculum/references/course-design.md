# Course design: the working basics

## Backward design (plan in this order)

1. **Desired results.** What students will be able to do by the end.
2. **Evidence.** How the owner will see they can (a checkpoint per module).
3. **Learning plan.** Only now, the lessons and activities that get them there.

Starting from topics ("week 1: mindset") produces a list of things covered.
Starting from results produces a course people finish with something done.

## Outcome verbs

Use verbs you can see a student do:

| Level | Verbs |
|---|---|
| Recall | list, name, identify |
| Explain | describe, explain, summarize |
| Apply | use, practice, demonstrate, draft, complete |
| Analyze | compare, sort, map, break down |
| Decide | choose, prioritize, judge, defend |
| Create | design, write, build, plan, pitch |

Avoid: understand, learn, know, appreciate, explore, "get clarity on"
(unless it's paired with something checkable: "get clarity on your next
role by choosing one to test").

Coaching courses usually aim at Apply, Decide and Create: the student
leaves having *done* something.

## Lesson shape (for live group calls)

| Part | Share of time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Open | ~10% | Check-in, the goal of today |
| Teach | ~30% | The owner's framework or step, one idea at a time |
| Practice | ~40% | Students do it: worksheet, pairs, hot seat, draft |
| Close | ~20% | Share, the action step for this week, Q&A |

A lesson that's all teaching and no practice rarely produces the
checkpoint. For recorded lessons: 5–15 minutes each, one idea, one action.

## Pacing

- One module outcome per week is plenty for a working adult.
- Homework should fit the time students have; if the audience is busy
  professionals, 30–60 minutes a week is a realistic default (suggested,
  owner decides).
- Build in a catch-up or integration point in longer programs (e.g. mid-
  point), and a final session that pulls it together.

## Promises

- Promise the doing: plans written, decisions made, first steps taken.
- Don't promise outcomes that depend on others (job offers, clients,
  income) or any health, legal or financial result.
- Client results the owner has seen can appear as their real stories,
  with what's typical, never as a guarantee.
